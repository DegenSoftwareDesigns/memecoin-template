# Brand guidelines

Status: unconfigured. Nothing below is approved until explicitly marked approved with a date and approver. Keep preferences, proposals and approved decisions distinct. Folder placement alone does not imply approval.

## Brand discovery

Use this process when the campaign has no agreed visual direction or the user wants to revisit it. Keep the conversation in the user's language and capture useful findings in this guide in English. Reuse answers already given; ask only what is needed for the next decision.

1. **Understand the idea.** Invite the user to describe the meme, story or joke, who it is for, and the feeling they want people to have. Ask whether a name or character already exists and which details are fixed versus open. A rough idea is enough; do not require a completed brief.
2. **Invite visual inspiration.** Ask the user to attach a few images of the desired look and feel: art, memes, characters, posters, textures, typography or other brands. Ask what they like in each and what to avoid. If they have no images, offer a small set of visual directions described in words and help them choose references; do not make uploads a prerequisite. If an image or link cannot be inspected, ask for an accessible image or a description instead of guessing its contents.
3. **Interpret together.** Summarize the useful signals: mood, palette, shapes, texture, typography, humor and possible character treatment. Separate the user's preferences from your interpretation. When references conflict, propose a few distinct directions and ask which to explore before assembling the board. Inspiration from another brand is a visual cue, not permission to copy its identity or reuse its artwork in campaign outputs.
4. **Review a visual moodboard.** Assemble a compact board from the available references, grouped and annotated to explain the proposed direction. Include a provisional palette and type direction where useful; label them as proposals. Show the board in the conversation for review, saving a reusable board as `Branding and references/moodboard.png` only when actually created. Save source images there only when needed, with simple names such as `reference-01.png`; record source credits and intended use below. If the assistant cannot assemble or display a board, explain that limitation and agree how to review the references visually. A written description alone is not a completed visual moodboard. Paid generation follows the approval rule in `AGENTS.md`.
5. **Approve the direction.** Ask what to keep, change or remove, then revise until the user explicitly approves the direction. Record the selected board or references and approval below. Approval of a moodboard establishes a direction; individual logos, avatars, character designs and fonts still need their own review.
6. **Build the identity.** Use the approved direction to develop only the assets the campaign currently needs. Fill the sections below as decisions are made, leaving unresolved fields as TBD. Add approved reusable assets to `Brand assets/`, character references to `Character/`, and approved generation inputs to the manifest. A moodboard's third-party images do not automatically become approved generation inputs.
